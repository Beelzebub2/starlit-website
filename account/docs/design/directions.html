Starlit account · design exploration

Three directions, judged by real tasks

Each direction uses only the Starlit website palette and type. None reuses another product's layout. Wireframes show 1440 px (left) and 390 px (right). The chosen direction is outlined.

Direction A · chosen

Ledger

Editorial single column with a quiet left rail. Data reads like a ledger: hairline rows, mono numbers, one sentence of context per row. The active profile is a persistent scope bar.

  • Profile scope always visible: lowest risk of editing the wrong profile.
  • Calm, scannable; counts are text, not coloured tiles.
  • Editors open as full routes with a sticky save bar.

Direction B

Profile constellation

Profiles are the home screen, like the app's "who's watching" picker. Pick a profile, then tabs for its data. Account items sit in a separate tab.

  • Familiar from the app; strong profile identity.
  • Account tasks (sessions, installations) feel buried.
  • Tab strip overflows on phones with 8+ data types.

Direction C

Workbench

Three panes: data type, item list, detail editor. Dense and fast for power users; on phones it becomes a drill-down stack.

  • Fast bulk edits, but three levels of context on a phone.
  • Profile scope is easy to lose in a dense layout.
  • Collections need more room than a third pane.

Decision

Ledger (A), with B's explicit profile scope. The main tasks are profile-scoped: fix a resume point, reorder addons, tidy collections, check where you are signed in. The biggest risk is editing the wrong profile or overwriting a change from another device. A puts the profile in a persistent scope bar, keeps counts as readable text, gives long editors (collections, settings) a whole route with a sticky save bar, and collapses to a single column on phones without hiding account tasks.
